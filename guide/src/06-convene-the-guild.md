# Convening the Guild

This is Workflow 1, the heart of the app: the critics read your chapter, and
the editor-in-chief turns their notes into a list you can act on.

## Step 1: Call the critics

On a chapter, click **Convene the Guild**.

```figure
convene-dialog
```

1. Tick the critics you want (A). Every enabled critic is ticked to begin with.
   Writers without the critic role, or disabled ones, do not appear.
2. Click **Convene** (B).

Your chapter is saved first, so the critics read exactly what you see.

## Step 2: Watch them read

The Guild panel opens beside the editor.

```figure
guild-panel
```

- Each critic gets a card (A) and reads at the same time as the others. While a
  critic works, you see its words arrive. When it finishes, the card shows its
  overall impression and up to three **issues** (B): a severity (high, medium,
  low), the exact passage it means, what the problem is, and a suggested fix.
- **Story bible conflicts** (C) list passages that contradict the bible.
- If a critic fails, for instance because the gateway is busy, its card says so
  and the others carry on. A critic whose reply did not follow the rules is
  asked once more automatically.
- **Cancel** (D) stops the session. You can close the tab and come back; the
  panel picks the session up where it was.
- A long chapter is split into scenes at scene breaks and headings and read
  scene by scene; the panel says so.

Click any quoted passage (E) and the editor scrolls to it and highlights it.

## Step 3: The editor-in-chief

When the critics are done, the editor-in-chief reads all their notes, merges
the ones that say the same thing, settles the ones that contradict each other,
drops notes that would change your story rather than improve the telling, and
puts what is left in order of importance. Its list appears at the top of the
panel; the critics' cards fold away underneath. The next section is about that
list.

If the editor-in-chief itself fails, the panel says so and shows the critics'
notes unmerged, most severe first, so you still have something to decide on.
