# Glossary

**Account.** Your own private workspace: projects, chapters, story bible,
writers, runs, settings and stats. Nobody else, not even the admin, sees inside
it.

**Admin.** A person who can add and manage accounts. Everyone else is an
*author*.

**Alias.** The name of a model on the gateway. Every writer has one; the app
never names models directly.

**Bible keeper.** The system agent that proposes story bible updates after a
revision.

**Compare.** The same co-writing request sent to two or three writers at once,
shown side by side.

**Co-write.** Asking one writer to draft text for you, from a selection or from
the cursor.

**Convene the Guild.** Asking the critics to read a chapter.

**Critic.** A writer with the critic role; it reads chapters and raises issues.

**Decision.** Your verdict on a note: accepted, rejected, or edited and
accepted. Also your verdict on a draft or a bible proposal.

**Draft.** Text a co-writer wrote for you, waiting to be inserted, used to
replace a selection, or discarded.

**Editor-in-chief.** The system agent that merges the critics' notes into one
prioritized list.

**Estimated cost (est.).** A cost worked out from token counts and the
gateway's prices because the reply carried no exact figure.

**Gateway.** The service all model requests go through. It knows the aliases
and reports costs.

**Guild panel.** The panel beside the editor where critiques, the editor's
list, revisions, drafts and bible proposals appear.

**Hunk.** One change in a revision, shown before and after; you can accept
hunks one by one.

**Issue (note).** One thing a critic or the editor-in-chief says about a
passage: severity, the exact quote, the problem, a suggested fix.

**Lead writer.** The system agent that applies the notes you accepted.

**Project.** One novel.

**Revision.** The lead writer's proposed changes to a chapter, reviewed change
by change before anything is applied.

**Run.** One piece of work the Guild did: a critique, a revision, a bible
update, a co-write, a compare, a writer test. Every run is kept in the history
with its cost.

**Scene.** A part of a chapter between scene breaks or headings. Long chapters
are read scene by scene.

**Scene token limit.** The size above which a chapter is split into scenes
before the writers read it. On the Settings page.

**Snapshot (version).** A saved copy of a chapter's text at one moment.

**Stale.** A critique or revision made against a version of the chapter that
has since changed.

**Story bible.** The book's fact sheet: premise, characters, setting, timeline,
style rules and chapter summaries.

**System prompt.** The description of the author a writer should emulate.
The Guild adds fixed rules underneath it.

**Temperature.** How adventurous a writer's model is: lower is steadier, higher
is wilder.

**Token.** The unit models read and write in, roughly three quarters of a word.
Costs are counted in tokens.

**Writer.** A voice in the Guild: a name, a system prompt, an alias, and roles.
