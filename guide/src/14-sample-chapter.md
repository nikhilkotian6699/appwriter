# A sample chapter to paste

If you have nothing of your own yet, paste this into a new chapter and convene
the Guild on it. It is deliberately imperfect, so the critics will have things
to say. It is original text written for this guide; use it freely.

> **The Lamp on Harrow Street**
>
> The lamp above the chemist's door had been burning for three days, and
> nobody on Harrow Street would admit to lighting it.
>
> Nell Carver noticed it on the Tuesday, coming home with the ledger under her
> arm and rain in her shoes. It was an old gas lamp, the kind the council had
> promised to take down before the war and then forgotten, and she had never
> once seen it lit. Now it glowed a soft, stubborn yellow in the daylight, which
> was somehow worse than at night.
>
> "It's a fault," said Mr. Adeyemi, who ran the chemist's and should have
> known. "Faults happen."
>
> "Faults don't pay their own gas bill," Nell said.
>
> He laughed, but he looked at the lamp the way you look at a dog that has
> stopped barking.
>
> * * *
>
> By Thursday the lamp had a visitor. A boy of about ten stood under it every
> evening at six, hands in his pockets, looking up. He did not go into the
> chemist's. He did not speak to anyone. When Nell asked him what he was
> waiting for, he considered the question seriously and then said, "I'm not
> sure yet," and she found that she believed him.
>
> She began to leave work early. She told herself it was the rain.
>
> On Friday the boy was not there, and the lamp was out, and Mr. Adeyemi was
> standing in his doorway with his coat on, although the shop was still open
> and it was nowhere near time to close. He said he had been meaning to go
> for a walk. He said it twice. Nell waited with him under the dark lamp
> until it was fully night, and neither of them said what they were waiting
> for, because they both knew, and because saying it would have made it true.

Suggested story bible entries to go with it, so the critics have something to
check the text against: a character entry for **Nell Carver** (a bookkeeper,
practical, dry, notices things), one for **Mr. Adeyemi** (runs the chemist's,
kind, hiding something), a setting entry for **Harrow Street** (a small town
high street, present day, the lamp is an old gas lamp), and a style rule
(**past tense, close third person on Nell, no explaining the uncanny**).
